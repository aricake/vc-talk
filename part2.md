# PMF
Product Market Fit

Startups can be split into 2 groups:

- Pre-PMF
- Post-PMF

---

# PMF
The absence of questions

If you're building something new, you don't know whether someone event cares.

Dentist / Plumber / Recruiter / ... ==> Demand / Need is well-known.

# Pre-PMF
Angel, Seed, Series A/B

- While you don't have PMF, all you do is trying to find PMF
- Investors get this
  - d.H. changing your idea / business ("pivoting") is generally possible
- Main goal: "create value" - "make the world a better place"

---

# Post-PMF
Series C, D, E, F => Growth

- You understand the levers
- You understand how to get customers easily
- Goal:
  - "capture value" (get rich)
  - market dominance

Test for PMF: "If you needed $1mn more revenue, could you do that easily?"

==> That's where all pre-PMF startups wanna go.

==> "Scaling" is a good problem to have.

---

# Who is worthy?

**In the end:** VCs give money to whoever they want to.

But:

- Post PMF:
  - metrics ("do you make money?" "are you capturing the market?")

- Pre PMF
  1. Problem<br>
    a. Potential Solution
  2. Timing ("Why now?")
  3. Team
  4. Traction
  5. Opportunity ("How much $$$")

==> The overall story is more important than *any* invidivual factor!!!!

# Important thought

If a VC is better at judging and evaluating, they get higher yields.

VCs with higher yields get more invesment and invest more.

==> Their criteria seem arbitrary, but they believe them to be the most successful set.

# Takeaways

<style>
ol {
  list-style-type: decimal;
  padding-left: 2rem;
}
</style>

1. A few companies were responsible for majority of VC profits.

2. Conservative companies ("safe bets") aren't worth it

3. It's hard to predict who's more successful, which makes safe bets even less attractive.

4. Only companies with the chance of outsized profits get VC investment.

5. After growth (where creating value > $$$), the focus shifts to capturing $$$ (getting rich).
